    About the Centre

    The Centre for Comparative Labour Studies was established in 1993 to provide an interdisciplinary focus for the study of questions relating ot labour. This includes the study of paid and unpaid labour, the past and the present, the local and the general. The Centre is committed to a consideration of comparative research relating to labour, based on class, race and gender.

    The Centre is located at the University of Warwick, which is an internationally recognised centre of excellence in the field of labour studies, industrial relations, and related areas.

    Local Context

    The Midlands is an appropriate area for such a Centre, consisting of one of the largest conurbations outside London. It is an industrial centre which has undergone many dramatic changes throughout its history. Many of the largest trade unions in the country have regional offices in the area, as do employer federations. Its ethnically diverse population makes this one of the most interesting and attractive areas in the country.

    International Context

    The Centre is internationally focused, engaged in projects concerned with labour studies in Russia and Eastern Europe, Western Europe, Australasia, Latin American and Africa. It maintains contact with researchers in all these areas and has a well developed visitors programme. Links have been established with similar centres in Russia, Malta, Western Europe, Australasia, Africa, and the Caribean.

    Research Interests

    The Centre acts as an intellectual forum for research, providing:

    • working groups on a range of research topics
    • seminars
    • conferences
    • working papers
    • publications

    It brings together researchers and students from different backgrounds and disciplines who work together in collaborative and supportive ways.

    Staff at the Centre are exceptionally well qualified to conduct a wide range of research work and labour consultancy, including:

    • preparation of submissions to public bodies
    • the analysis of policy options
    • surveys
    • case-study research
    • briefings

    It has five main areas of research work:

    • Economic and public policies
    • Politics and restructuring in Russia and Eastern Europe
    • Workplace studies
    • Labour market studies
    • Globalisation and labour regulation

    The Centre has access to a wide range of research facilities at the University of Warwick and has been successful in attracting considerable funding to support its activities.

    Membership

    The membership of the Centre is highly qualified and is drawn from a number of disciplines, including:

    • Applied Social Studies
    • Economics
    • Industrial Relations
    • Law
    • Politics
    • Women and Gender Studies
    • Sociology

    In addition, there is an active involvement in the work of the Centre by the Library which has an unrivalled collection of labour documentary and archival material. This includes the acclaimed Modern Records Centre.

    The Centre has close links with other similar centres in Britain and overseas as well as with the major unions in Britain. Members of the Centre work with individual trade unions, trade union federations, government departments and international agencies.

    The Centre provides a particularly stimulating and well resourced environment for visitors, research students, and practitioners. It hosts a large number of visiting research fellows who are able to develop their research interests in a supportive intellectual environment.

    Teaching Programmes

    The members of the Centre are skilled graduate and post-graduate teachers in the area of labour studies. There are relevant courses on offer in Labour Studies, Industrial Relations, Women and Gender Studies, Labour Economics and Labour Markets, and Labour History.
